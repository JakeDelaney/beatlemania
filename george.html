GEORGE HARRISON

George Harrison, born on February 25, 1943, in Liverpool, England, was the lead guitarist of The Beatles. He joined the band in 1960 and became known for his distinctive guitar playing and contributions to the band's sound. Harrison also became a key songwriter, creating memorable songs like "Here Comes the Sun" and "Something."

After The Beatles disbanded in 1970, Harrison enjoyed a successful solo career. His album All Things Must Pass (1970) was both a critical and commercial success, with hits like "My Sweet Lord." He also co-founded the Concert for Bangladesh in 1971, one of the first large-scale charity events.

Harrison was deeply influenced by Indian philosophy, which shaped both his music and personal life. He passed away from cancer on November 29, 2001, at the age of 58, leaving behind a legacy of impactful music and spiritual influence.

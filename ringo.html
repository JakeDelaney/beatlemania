RINGO STARR

Ringo Starr, born Richard Starkey on July 7, 1940, in Liverpool, England, is best known as the drummer for The Beatles. He joined the band in 1962, replacing Pete Best. Starr's steady drumming style became an essential part of The Beatles' sound, and he occasionally contributed vocals, most famously on "With a Little Help from My Friends" and "Octopus's Garden."

After The Beatles disbanded in 1970, Starr pursued a solo career with moderate success. His 1973 hit "Photograph" became one of his signature songs, and he also formed the All-Starr Band, a group that toured with various other musicians. Ringo remained active in the music scene, recording albums and performing live.

In addition to his music career, Starr has acted in films, appearing in several movies, including A Hard Day's Night and Help! He is known for his positive, easy-going personality and his advocacy for peace and love. Starr continues to perform and remain active in the music world, with a legacy as one of rock's most beloved drummers.
